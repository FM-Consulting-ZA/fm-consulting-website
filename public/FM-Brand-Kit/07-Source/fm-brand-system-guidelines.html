The mark

One drawing, three optical sizes

The monogram reads as a figure before it reads as initials — head, outstretched arm, spine, stance. That is deliberate: the product here is one operator's judgement, not a firm's letterhead. The head opens on the spine's axis, the F's crossbar floats free, and a single stroke serves as both the F's stem and the M's first leg.

Because the drawing is fine, it does not survive reduction as a single file. Three cuts exist. Match the cut to the size it will be reproduced at, never to the size of the file on your screen. Full construction, grid and reduction tests are in the mark construction sheet.

Lockups

Three arrangements, fixed spacing

The wordmark is Archivo Expanded, set in caps at a cap height of 2.2 w with +0.18 em tracking, and supplied as outlines so no artwork ever depends on a font being installed. The gap between mark and wordmark is 3 w and does not change. In the horizontal lockup the wordmark is centred on the mark's cap-to-foot band, not on its bounding box — the descender would otherwise drag it low.

Clear space

2 w on every side — the mark's own internal void, repeated outside it. Nothing sits inside that box: no text, no rule, no image edge. In a circular avatar, centre optically rather than by bounding box; the descender and the left-reaching arm pull the geometric centre down and to the left.

Colour

Ink and paper. That is the palette.

There is no accent colour, and adding one later is the single fastest way to make this system look like everyone else's. Monochrome is not a limitation here — it is the only palette in which a hairline monoline mark stays the most confident thing on the page. Hierarchy is carried by weight, scale and space.

Both the ink and the paper carry the same faint green-grey cast, so printed and digital surfaces read as one material rather than a cold screen white beside a borrowed black. The three tints exist for rules, panels and secondary text — never for emphasis.

Typography

One grotesque, one monospace

Archivo carries the voice. Its width axis gives the wordmark wide-set, engineered caps without introducing a third typeface. IBM Plex Mono carries anything countable — figures, addresses, reference numbers, table labels — which is most of what a consultancy's documents actually contain. Both are open-licence and ship with this kit.

400 / 500 / 600. Caps only, +0.18 em. The wordmark, cover statements, and nothing else. It is a signature, not a text face.

Stationery

Core stationery

One margin governs every piece. The card front is a full ink field carrying nothing but the monogram; everything a recipient needs to read sits on the back, on the same left margin as the letterhead. Card and compliments slip carry 3 mm bleed with crop marks; the letterhead and envelope are supplied at exact size so they can go through an office printer unmodified.

Client documents

Proposal, invoice, case study

The document system is where the monospace earns its place. Fees, dates, references and metrics are all set in Plex Mono so they align down the page without tabs or hand-kerning, and so a number always looks like a number rather than like prose. Sample copy is real enough to stress the layout — replace it, but keep the structure.

Presentation

Eight slides, and a rule for each

Ink for the cover, dividers and close; paper for everything in between — so a deck is bracketed rather than uniform. The mono eyebrow at the top of every content slide is the navigation. There is no accent stripe, no underline beneath a title, and no chart colour that is not a tint of the ink.

If you cannot write the takeaway as a sentence, the chart is not ready to be shown.

Digital

Avatars, covers, favicons, signature

The icon cut is optically centred in every badge — the geometric centre would sit low and left. The 16 px favicon is not a scaled vector but a hand-set pixel glyph, because no outline survives a sixteen-pixel box. The email signature is supplied twice: once with the lockup as an embedded image, and once as type only, for clients that strip images.

Rules

Six ways to break it

Never re-colour

The system is ink and paper. A tinted mark is a different brand.

Never stretch or rotate

The stroke unit governs every distance in the drawing.

Never outline the mark

It re-closes the aperture and doubles every void.

Never place on a busy image

If a photograph is unavoidable, lay an ink field under the mark.

Never rebuild the lockup

Spacing is fixed at 3 w. Use the supplied files.

Never mix the cuts

One optical size per surface. Match the cut to the reproduction size.
